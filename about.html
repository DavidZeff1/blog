About

Checking the numbers

I'm David Zeff. I work as a budget analyst — תחשיבן — in the social services department of the Gush Etzion Regional Council, and I live in Alon Shvut.

Most of my week is spent inside public budgets: reconciling ministry transfers, forecasting departmental spend, running procurement processes, and building the small tools that make all of that less painful than it would otherwise be. It is unglamorous work and I find it genuinely interesting, largely because budgets are where stated priorities meet actual ones.

I studied computer science at Sapir College. That background shows up here mostly as a habit: when a claim has a number attached, I want to see the number, find out where it came from, and recompute it myself.

What this site is for

Essays about economics, public policy, and the data underneath both — usually Israeli, not always. The rules I'm holding myself to:

  • Every figure is sourced, and the source is named in the chart, not buried.
  • Every chart is built from a dataset in the public repository, so anyone can check my arithmetic.
  • Anything illustrative is labelled illustrative.
  • The strongest objection to my own argument goes in the piece, not in the comments.

If I get something wrong, I would like to know. Corrections are welcome and will be made visibly.

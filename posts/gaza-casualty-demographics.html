Not Indiscriminate: What Hamas's Own Numbers Show

The Gaza Health Ministry's named casualty lists carry an age and sex distribution that does not match the population they came from.

By David Zeff

21 August 20266 min readGaza MoH named lists

"Genocide." "Indiscriminate." "Disproportionate." We've been hearing these words for a long time now. Wikipedia describes the war in Gaza as "a genocide, agreed upon by most scholars." Sounds damning, doesn't it?

To back up these accusations, Amnesty International, South Africa, and other supposedly "impartial" parties say they have rigorous, substantial evidence. What is that evidence? Several unrelated witnesses, all of whom claim there were no military targets in the area that was struck. How would they know? Did all of these very unbiased—and certainly not hostile to Israel—witnesses happen to live in that exact apartment and know for a fact that there were no military targets inside? Very convincing.

So here is my own evidence—drawn from Hamas's own numbers, the very numbers used to accuse us.

I downloaded all ten lists the Gaza Health Ministry has published since January 2024 and ran the distributions myself. The most recent contains 60,199 named records, each with a national ID number, a date of birth, and a sex.

If bombs fell on a population without regard for who was beneath them, the dead would be a random sample of the living—and a random sample resembles the population it came from. Gaza's dead do not resemble Gaza.

Children are 44 percent of the population, but only 25 percent of the list. Men aged 25 to 54 are 14 percent of the population, but 33 percent of the list. Had those 60,199 deaths been drawn at random, roughly 26,500 of them would be children. Fewer than 15,000 are.

Consider the sex ratio. A boy and a girl of the same age in Gaza live in the same building and shelter in the same room. A collapsing structure does not sort them, and the birth sex ratio—about 1.05 boys per girl—is among the most stable figures in demography. Under indiscriminate killing, the ratio of male to female deaths should be flat at 1.05 at every age. In the Ministry's own data, it sits near 1.1 through early childhood, then climbs steadily from around age ten to above 3.0 in the twenties. Add to this the fact that Hamas actively recruits children into its ranks—a war crime in its own right, by the way.

I'm not claiming every military-aged male is a militant. But the simple fact remains: if the bombing were truly systemic and indiscriminate, the data would show a very specific pattern. Put gender and age bins on the x-axis and death count on the y-axis, and the chart should closely mirror the same chart with population count on the y-axis instead. Yet, by Hamas's own numbers, these two charts are night and day from each other.

That makes the case, beyond any reasonable doubt, that Israel's actions could not have been indiscriminate. And if they were not indiscriminate, it follows that there could not have been any intent to destroy the people of Gaza, in whole or in part.

Sources and method. All figures are computed directly from the Gaza Health Ministry's published named casualty lists, archived at gazadeaths.org. Population shares are computed against a published Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics projection, using the same five-year age bands as the casualty counts so that the numerator and denominator carry identical cut-offs.

The counts behind every chart on this page are in assets/gaza-data.js in the public repository, along with the number of records excluded for a missing or unparseable date of birth or sex. Anyone can recompute the arithmetic.
